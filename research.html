Housing Like Irish Weather?

Undergraduate thesis, UCD School of Economics, April 2026. Supervised by Prof. Eoghan O'Neill.

Volatility regimes and asymmetric transmission in the Irish housing market. The question: does demand feed through to housing differently in calm and stressed times?

Identification. Regimes are identified from the demand block (modified domestic demand, rent-to-income, unemployment and CPI) rather than from house prices, so the thing that defines a regime is kept separate from the outcomes it is meant to explain.

The 𝔍𝔄 prior. A hierarchical Student-t shrinkage prior for Markov-switching VARs. Shrinkage intensity τA, lag decay φ and tail thickness νA are estimated jointly with the coefficients, and it nests the Minnesota prior as a limiting case (νA → ∞, φ → 1).

The inference. A differentiable Hamilton filter inside the No-U-Turn Sampler marginalises the regime sequence at every leapfrog step, so regime uncertainty flows through every stage instead of being frozen as a generated regressor.

What I found

Ninety-eight quarters of Irish data. The asset block, not house prices themselves, is where the regime story lives.

28 quarters

One high-volatility episode in the asset block, 2008Q2 to 2015Q1. Five times longer than the matching demand episode, consistent with a financial accelerator keeping stress alive long after the shock.

About 2×

The credit block's response to the shadow rate is roughly twice as large in the high-volatility regime, but stress there is short-lived: a two to three quarter window for policy.

5.09×

Construction cost volatility amplifies by this factor in the high-volatility regime, while quantities don't follow. Supply is held back by non-price constraints.

36.6%

The long-run probability of the high-volatility regime in the asset block. House prices themselves show only modest, imprecise regime separation.

The policy point: macroprudential tools calibrated on single-regime estimates, or triggered by price thresholds, risk being miscalibrated in exactly the periods where calibration matters most.
